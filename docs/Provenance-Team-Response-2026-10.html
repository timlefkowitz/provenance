01Product Positioning & Messaging

The registrar for every collection

We love your "registrar" framing and want to adopt it. A registrar is the person at a museum who keeps the official record of every object: what it is, where it is, who owned it, where it has been shown. Most galleries, artists and private collectors don't have one. They have spreadsheets, PDFs and email. Provenance gives them one.

Draft 30-second overview (for the team to refine together)

Elevator pitch · v1

Provenance is the registrar for your collection — whether you're an artist, a gallery or a private collector. You catalog your works once, issue a certificate with a scannable QR code, and every sale, loan and exhibition is added to a history that can't be quietly rewritten. It replaces the spreadsheets, PDFs and email threads that the art world runs on today with one trusted record that travels with the artwork.

Shorter version for a conversation at a gallery opening: "We're the registrar for your collection. Every work gets a verified record and a history that follows it from studio to sale."

Your questions, answered as we see them today

QuestionOur answer
What problem does Provenance solve, and for whom? An artwork's value depends on its documented history: who made it, who owned it, where it was shown. That history usually lives in scattered files and in people's memories, so it gets lost, disputed or faked. Provenance keeps it in one place for galleries (inventory, shows, sales), artists (proof of authorship and career record) and collectors (proof of ownership).
Why choose Provenance over what they use today? Today's options are either a spreadsheet, a general inventory tool (ArtLogic, Artwork Archive, Arternal) or a certificate-only product (Verisart and others). We combine both: the day-to-day inventory and operations a gallery needs, plus certificates that the artist, gallery and collector each confirm. One tool instead of three, and the record can be shown to a buyer, insurer or museum.
What makes it distinct or hard to copy?
  • Patent pending on our certificate model, where artist, gallery and collector each confirm their part of the record (a provisional application is on file).
  • History you can defend. Changes are recorded as events, and third parties propose corrections that the owner approves rather than editing silently.
  • Network effect. Every certificate links an artist, a gallery and a collector. Each new gallery brings its artists and buyers onto the platform.
  • Breadth already built: native iOS app, QR scanning, find-an-artwork-by-photo, 20+ website templates, operations and CRM. That's ten months and nearly 800 product updates a competitor would have to repeat.
What is our strongest initial market? Galleries — especially established independent galleries — with museums and institutions next. Galleries feel the pain every day, they pay for software already, and each one brings its artists and collectors with it. Tim's own gallery (25 years in operation) is our first real-world test site. Artists stay free or low-cost: they are the supply side and a big part of our mission, but not where most revenue comes from.

Next 90 days — make it ready for real galleries Committed

  1. Inventory import. Upload a spreadsheet, PDF or photo of an existing inventory list and get draft records to review — no emails or notifications sent during import. First run: Tim's gallery's 25-year archive. This is what makes Founding Gallery onboarding take an afternoon instead of weeks.
  2. Tamper-proof history. Every change to a work's record is logged permanently, even if the work is later deleted. Owners can choose whether the history is public.
  3. Reliability. Automatic alerts when payments, certificate emails or imports fail; automated tests on every change.
  4. Privacy & trust paperwork. A list of the services we share data with, a standard data agreement, and a per-gallery switch to turn AI features off.
  5. Accessibility review of the certificate, collection and import screens, plus the accessibility statement museums ask for.

  • Gallery and institution pricing above $990/yr, with invoicing.
  • Museum loan and donation workflows — designed with the first museum that's in active conversation.
  • Partner verification API for insurers, auction houses and marketplaces (keys and permissions already exist; the public endpoints come next).
  • Procurement basics institutions ask for: single sign-on, security questionnaire, uptime commitment.

Parked on purpose

Real estate and other non-art verticals (cut), AI research into unknown artworks, insurance and appraisal partnerships, expanded grants. Each has a clear trigger for coming back — for example, a paying institution asking for it.
